# Code of Conduct

site2api is built in the open by people with different backgrounds, languages and levels of
experience. We want taking part to be a good experience for everyone, and this document says
what that means in practice.

## Our pledge

We, as contributors and maintainers, commit to making participation in this project free of
harassment for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education,
socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual
identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new, or who write in a second language.
- Discuss ideas, not people. Disagree with a proposal by explaining what you would do instead.
- Give and accept feedback gracefully, and say thank you.
- Take responsibility when you get something wrong, apologize to those affected, and learn from it.
- Focus on what is best for the people who use site2api and for the project as a whole.

## What we do not accept

- Sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Insults, personal or political attacks, trolling, and deliberately derogatory comments.
- Harassment in public or in private, including continued contact after being asked to stop.
- Publishing someone else's private information (an address, an email, a real name they have not
  shared, or a recording of their traffic) without their explicit permission.
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

Because site2api works with recorded browser traffic, one more rule applies to issues, pull
requests and discussions: **never post a HAR file, a cookie, a token or an API key**, yours or
anyone else's. Maintainers will delete such content and may hide the thread.

## Scope

This code applies in every project space (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, chat
channels) and when someone represents the project in public, for example by posting from an
official account or acting as a representative at an event.

## Reporting

If you see or experience behavior that breaks this code, report it privately to the maintainer,
[@flyingsquirrel0419](https://github.com/flyingsquirrel0419), using the contact on their GitHub
profile. Include links or screenshots where you can. Reports are handled in confidence, and the
reporter's privacy and safety come first.

## Enforcement

Maintainers decide on a response that fits what happened, usually one of:

1. **Correction**: a private note explaining what was inappropriate, and possibly a request for
   a public apology.
2. **Warning**: a warning with consequences for continued behavior, and no interaction with the
   people involved for a set time.
3. **Temporary ban**: no interaction with the project or its community for a set time.
4. **Permanent ban**: no interaction with the project or its community, permanently.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this code in good faith may face consequences decided by
other members of the project's leadership.
